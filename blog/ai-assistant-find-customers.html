Can My AI Assistant Find Customers for Me?

📅 October 27, 2026 ⏱️ 4 min read ✍️ Oleg Lychkovski

Short answer: for some of the work, yes. On its own, an AI assistant like Claude or ChatGPT knows what it learned in training and what a quick web search turns up. Connect an app to it, and it can do real research for you: make a list of the businesses you want to sell to, with their phone numbers, or find the YouTubers and opinion leaders your customers already watch, with a way to reach each one. What it can’t do is decide who is right for you, or build the relationship. That part stays yours.

Here are two jobs most business owners have, what to type, and what comes back.

Job 1: A List of the Businesses You Want to Sell To

Suppose you run a landscaping company in Minnesota. Homeowners keep you busy all summer, but the steady, year-round work is commercial: office parks, apartment complexes, retail centers. Those contracts often go through property managers. You don’t need more ads. You need the property managers near you, and how to reach them.

With an app like Business Finder connected, you type one sentence:

“Use Business Finder on Apify to list the property management companies in Minneapolis and St. Paul, highest-reviewed first, with phone, website and the contact from each company’s website.”

You get one row per company, as Google Maps lists it: its category, phone, website and rating. With the contact check on, the app also reads each company’s website for the owner’s email and messenger contacts, and says where it found each one. If a company publishes no contact, its row says so.

Two things make this better than searching by hand. The results come back as one table you can sort and share, not one pin at a time on a map. And the app searches in the local language too, so the same request works if your customers are in Warsaw or Monterrey.

Job 2: The YouTubers and Opinion Leaders Your Customers Watch

Suppose you sell skincare online in the US. Ads get scrolled past, but your buyers learn their routines from a handful of YouTubers and TikTok opinion leaders they trust. When those people recommend something, their viewers listen.

With Audience Intel connected, you describe your customers in your own words, and your assistant brings back a list of the opinion leaders they already follow, each with a way to reach them. The list is only as good as your description, so be specific: who your customers are, where they are, and who looks similar but isn’t a fit. For example:

“Use Audience Intel on Apify to find the YouTube and TikTok channels whose audience is women in the US building a skincare routine for themselves, not dermatologists or makeup artists, with their contacts.”

The next step is a conversation: a sponsored video, a joint campaign, or a revenue share where they earn from every customer they send you.

How to Connect an App

  • Claude: open Connectors, search for Apify and add it, then sign in to Apify once. If it isn’t listed for you, add a custom connector with the address https://mcp.apify.com.
  • ChatGPT: custom connections work in ChatGPT Business, Enterprise and Edu workspaces, added by an admin. Personal plans can’t add them.
  • No assistant: open the app on Apify and fill in the form.

Any of the three needs a free Apify account. Ask for an estimate first, and the app shows the price before it spends anything.

What It Won’t Do for You

An app finds people. It doesn’t decide which of them fit your business, and it doesn’t build the relationship. The lists hold what businesses and YouTubers publish about themselves, so a contact can be out of date. Read the list, pick the few that fit, and write to them the way you would to any new partner.

A Quick Checklist

  • Write down who you want to reach in one sentence: who they are, where they are, and who looks similar but isn’t a fit.
  • Connect the app to your assistant once.
  • Ask for an estimate, then run it.
  • Pick the five best fits from the list and write to them yourself.
